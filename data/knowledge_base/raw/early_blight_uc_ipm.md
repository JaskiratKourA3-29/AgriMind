# Tomato: Early Blight Management (UC IPM Guidelines)

## Disease Overview & Field Symptoms
Early blight is caused by the fungus *Alternaria solani*. Although called "early" blight, the disease frequently becomes severe later in the season as the tomato fruit load increases and nitrogen levels in the canopy begin to decline. Stressed or heavy-yielding vines are particularly susceptible. Leaf spots are leathery, brown, circular, and show dark concentric rings, resembling an archery target. Collar rot symptoms can girdle the stems of transplants. Fruit infections occur at the stem-end calyx attachment, forming a sunken black crater covered with dark velvety spores.

## Environmental Triggers & Epidemiology
- Optimum fungal growth occurs between 75°F and 85°F (24°C to 29°C).
- Prolonged wet periods (at least 6 to 8 hours of continuous leaf wetness from rain, sprinkler irrigation, or heavy coastal fog) are necessary for spore germination and host penetration.
- Spores survive for more than one year on buried plant residues and on alternative solanaceous hosts.

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM) Practices
1. **Resistant & Tolerant Cultivars:** When available, select tomato varieties with partial resistance or tolerance to *Alternaria solani* to slow down epidemic progress.
2. **Nutritional Balance:** Maintain adequate, balanced nitrogen fertilization throughout the growing season. Plants suffering nitrogen deficiency senesce prematurely and suffer accelerated early blight defoliation.
3. **Deep Plowing & Sanitation:** After harvest, deeply plow under crop residue to accelerate fungal decomposition by soil microorganisms. Eliminate volunteer solanaceous weeds around field borders.
4. **Avoid Overhead Irrigation:** In arid and semi-arid production zones, furrow or subsurface drip irrigation keeps the plant canopy dry and almost completely suppresses early blight development.

## Chemical Control Schedules
- Begin preventative fungicide applications when the first fruit cluster sets or when early blight symptoms are detected on lower leaves.
- **Protectant Treatments:** Chlorothalonil or mancozeb provide reliable preventative protection. Apply on a 7- to 14-day schedule depending on rainfall frequency.
- **Systemic Products:** When weather favors disease, incorporate translaminar and systemic materials such as pyraclostrobin (Cabrio - FRAC 11) or fluopyram + trifloxystrobin (Luna Sensation - FRAC 7/11).
- **Organic Options:** Certified organic formulations of copper hydroxide (Kocide 3000) or *Bacillus subtilis* (Serenade ASO) applied preventatively can slow early blight progress under moderate disease pressure.
