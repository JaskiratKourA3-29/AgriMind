# Management of Bacterial Spot on Tomato in Florida (UF/IFAS Extension)

## Overview & Pathogen Biology
Bacterial spot caused by *Xanthomonas perforans* is the most economically damaging foliar disease of fresh-market tomato production in humid subtropical and tropical environments. The pathogen attacks all above-ground plant parts including leaves, stems, flowers, and fruit. Severe defoliation results in major yield loss and exposes developing tomato fruit to solar injury (sunscald).

## Copper Resistance & Chemical Strategies
Widespread resistance to copper bactericides exists in field populations of *Xanthomonas perforans*:
1. **Copper plus Mancozeb Synergism:** Routine reliance on copper alone frequently fails due to copper-tolerant strains. The tank-mixture of fixed copper (copper hydroxide or copper octanoate) with mancozeb is critical to generate toxic levels of soluble copper ions that kill resistant bacteria.
2. **Plant Host Defense Activators:** Acibenzolar-S-methyl (Actigard) induces systemic acquired resistance (SAR) in tomato plants. To optimize efficacy and avoid plant stunting, apply Actigard at recommended low doses (0.33 to 0.75 oz/acre) starting 7 to 10 days post-transplant, repeating every 7 to 10 days for up to six to eight applications per season.
3. **Bacteriophage Formulations:** Bacteriophage bio-bactericides (AgriPhage) target specific strains of *Xanthomonas*. Apply bacteriophages in early morning or evening to minimize degradation by solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation. Do not tank-mix phages directly with concentrated copper compounds without testing compatibility.

## Biological Control Options
- **Microbial Antagonists:** Formulations of *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* (strain D747) or *Bacillus subtilis* provide moderate suppression of bacterial spot when applied preventatively as protective foliage coatings. Biologicals are especially valuable in organic tomato production systems where synthetic bactericides are prohibited.

## Post-Harvest Field Hygiene & Sanitation
- **Crop Residue Destruction:** Immediately disc, flail, or plow under tomato crop residues immediately following the final harvest. Rapid tissue decomposition degrades bacterial survival shelters in the soil.
- **Volunteer Control:** Actively eradicate volunteer tomato and pepper plants, as well as nightshade weed species (such as *Solanum americanum*), in field perimeters, ditch banks, and adjacent fallow fields between growing seasons.
