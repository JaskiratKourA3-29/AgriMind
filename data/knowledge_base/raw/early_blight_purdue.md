# Foliar Diseases of Tomato: Early Blight (Purdue Extension)

## Symptomology & Identification
Early blight is caused by the fungal pathogen *Alternaria solani*. Symptoms first emerge on the oldest, lowermost leaves as small, circular, brown to black spots. As spots expand to 1/4 to 1/2 inch in diameter, they develop distinctive concentric ridges resembling a target board or bullseye pattern. The tissue surrounding the lesion frequently becomes yellow (chlorotic). As lesions multiply, entire lower leaves turn brown, wither, and drop from the plant. This progressive defoliation works upward through the canopy, exposing fruit to sunscald. On stems, dark, sunken, oval cankers may form (termed collar rot when occurring at the soil line on young transplants). On green or ripe fruit, lesions occur near the stem attachment (calyx end) as dark, leathery, sunken, concentric depressions.

## Overwintering & Disease Cycle
*Alternaria solani* overwinters in infested tomato vines, potato debris, and wild solanaceous weeds (such as black nightshade and horsenettle). Spores (conidia) are produced abundantly during warm, humid weather and are disseminated by wind currents, rain splash, and mechanical equipment. Infection occurs rapidly when leaf surfaces remain wet from dew or rain for 8 or more hours at temperatures between 75°F and 85°F (24°C to 29°C).

## Cultural Management Guidelines
1. **Crop Rotation:** Practice a strict 2- to 3-year crop rotation out of solanaceous crops. Do not plant tomatoes where tomatoes, potatoes, eggplants, or peppers grew in the previous two seasons.
2. **Plant Spacing & Trellising:** Space plants adequately (18 to 24 inches within rows, 5 to 6 feet between rows) and stake or cage plants to promote rapid drying of foliage after dew and rain.
3. **Mulching & Pruning:** Apply straw, plastic mulch, or landscape fabric to provide a physical barrier preventing rain from splashing soil-borne fungal spores onto lower leaves. Prune lower suckers and leaves up to 12 inches above the mulch line once the plant is established.
4. **Watering Practices:** Use drip irrigation or soaker hoses. Avoid overhead irrigation; if overhead watering is unavoidable, irrigate early in the morning so sun and wind dry the leaves within 2 hours.

## Fungicide Spray Programs
- **Preventative Protectants:** Begin applications early in the season before disease appears or when first fruit cluster reaches walnut size:
  - Chlorothalonil (Bravo Weather Stik, Daconil at 1.5 to 2.0 pt/acre) or Mancozeb (Dithane, Manzate at 1.5 to 2.0 lb/acre) applied every 7 to 10 days.
- **Targeted Systemic Fungicides:** When disease pressure is high, alternate protectants with systemic fungicides:
  - Azoxystrobin (Quadris - FRAC 11), Boscalid (Endura - FRAC 7), or Difenoconazole / Cyprodinil (Inspire Super - FRAC 3/9).
  - Always rotate FRAC codes to prevent the development of fungicide resistance.
- **Organic Protectants:** Fixed copper fungicides (copper hydroxide) or sulfur products provide fair suppression when applied frequently before infection.
