# Late Blight Management in Tomato (Cornell CALS & USABlight)

## Diagnostic Features of Late Blight
Late blight is caused by the oomycete *Phytophthora infestans* (an organism related to brown algae rather than true fungi). It is an extremely destructive pathogen capable of completely destroying entire fields of tomato and potato crops within 7 to 10 days if left unmanaged.
- **Foliar Symptoms:** Initial lesions appear as pale green, water-soaked, irregular areas on leaves. Under humid conditions, lesions expand rapidly into large, purplish-brown to dark brown necrotic patches.
- **White Sporulation:** In damp, foggy, or rainy conditions (relative humidity > 90%), a characteristic white, delicate, cottony growth (sporangia and sporangiophores) forms on the undersides of infected leaves and along affected green stems.
- **Stem & Petiole Cankers:** Dark brown, greasy lesions frequently girdle petioles and green stems, causing rapid collapse of distal foliage.
- **Fruit Symptoms:** Green or ripe fruit develop large, firm, rough, dark brown to olive-colored greasy lesions that remain firm unless invaded by secondary bacterial soft rot organisms.

## Epidemiology & Weather Requirements
Unlike early blight and bacterial spot which prefer warm conditions, *Phytophthora infestans* thrives in cool, wet, humid weather:
- Ideal daytime temperatures: 60°F to 75°F (15°C to 24°C).
- Cool nighttime temperatures: 50°F to 60°F (10°C to 15°C) with fog, dew, or rain.
- Temperatures above 86°F (30°C) halt pathogen spore production and slow down lesion expansion.
- Millions of microscopic sporangia are produced per lesion and can be transported aloft by wind currents for 5 to 10 miles.

## Disease Forecasting & Scouting
- Monitor the national **USABlight** network and regional weather-based disease forecasting systems (such as BlightCast and NEWA).
- When weather conditions reach high-risk severity thresholds (accumulated Late Blight Disease Severity Values, DSVs), preventative fungicide coverage must be established immediately prior to rain events.

## Resistant Cultivars
Planting resistant tomato varieties offers durable genetic protection:
- Cultivars carrying both the *Ph-2* and *Ph-3* resistance genes (such as **Mountain Magic**, **Defiant PHR**, **Plum Regal**, and **Iron Lady**) exhibit exceptional resistance to the predominant USABlight clonal lineages (including US-23).

## Fungicide Recommendations
1. **Preventative Protectants:** Apply chlorothalonil, mancozeb, or copper fungicides on a 5- to 7-day interval before late blight is reported in the region.
2. **Targeted Oomycete Systemics:** Once late blight is confirmed locally or weather is conducive, transition to targeted translaminar or systemic oomycete fungicides:
   - **Cymoxanil (Curzate 60DF):** Possesses 24- to 48-hour curative / post-infection kickback activity; must be tank-mixed with a protectant (chlorothalonil or mancozeb).
   - **Mandipropamid (Revus):** High preventative and anti-sporulant efficacy.
   - **Fluopicolide (Presidio):** Systemic oomycide with excellent translaminar movement.
   - **Propamocarb (Previcur Flex):** Systemic protectant.
3. **Organic Production:** Fixed copper fungicides (such as Champ, Badge, or Cueva) provide preventative protection if leaf surfaces are completely coated prior to spore deposition. Copper has zero curative kickback activity once tissue is infected.
4. **Immediate Rogueing:** When an initial hot-spot plant is detected, bag the plant in plastic on a dry sunny midday, cut at the base, and remove from the field to extinguish the localized spore source.
