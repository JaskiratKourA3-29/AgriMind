# Tomato: Late Blight Management (UC IPM Guidelines)

## Symptoms and Field Diagnosis
*Phytophthora infestans* infects leaves, stems, and fruit of tomato. Foliar lesions begin as pale green, water-soaked spots that rapidly enlarge into dark brown to black blighted areas. When leaves are examined in the morning during periods of high humidity or wetness, a fine white moldy growth composed of sporangiophores and sporangia is visible on the underside of the leaves at the margins of the necrotic lesions. Stems develop dark brown cankers that can cause the collapse of entire branches. Green tomato fruit develop dark, firm, greasy-looking, rough brown lesions with distinct borders, which do not soften unless invaded by secondary rots.

## Pathogen Survival & Overwintering Reservoirs
Unlike fungal pathogens that form dormant resting structures in dry crop residue, *Phytophthora infestans* requires living host tissue to survive from one season to the next:
- It overwinters primarily in infected potato tubers (cull piles or unharvested volunteer potatoes) and in living volunteer tomato plants or solanaceous weeds in protected microclimates.
- Eliminating cull piles, volunteers, and nightshade species within a 1-mile radius of commercial tomato plantings significantly reduces early-season epidemic risk.

## Cultural Practices & Irrigation Management
1. **Certified Transplants:** Inspect all incoming tomato transplants thoroughly before setting into the field. Reject any flat showing suspicious water-soaked lesions or stem necrosis.
2. **Irrigation Selection:** Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation during periods of cool or cloudy weather. Drip irrigation keeps foliage completely dry, denying the pathogen the 6 to 8 hours of leaf wetness required for spore germination.
3. **Field Layout:** Orient planting rows parallel to prevailing winds to encourage air movement and rapid canopy drying.
4. **Sanitation:** Disinfect pruning shears, stakes, and implements with bleach or quaternary ammonium compounds between field blocks.

## Chemical Control & Spray Programs
- Fungicide applications must be initiated before infection occurs. Once late blight is established across more than 5% to 10% of the canopy, chemical control becomes ineffective.
- **Protectant Regimens:** Chlorothalonil (1.5 to 2.1 pt/acre), mancozeb (1.5 to 2.0 lb/acre), or fixed copper applied every 7 to 10 days under low-risk weather, and every 5 days under cool, overcast, rainy conditions.
- **Translaminar / Systemic Regimens:** During active disease pressure, tank-mix protectants with specialized oomycide chemistry such as cymoxanil, dimethomorph (Acrobat), or mandipropamid. Always observe strict label restrictions on maximum applications per season to delay resistance development.
