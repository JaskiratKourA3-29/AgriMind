# Bacterial Spot and Speck of Tomato (Cornell CALS)

## Diagnostic Symptomology
Bacterial spot of tomato is caused by several species of *Xanthomonas*, predominantly *Xanthomonas perforans* and *Xanthomonas campestris pv. vesicatoria*. Foliar symptoms begin as small, water-soaked, circular to irregular lesions (less than 1/8 inch or 3 mm in diameter) on leaves. As lesions age, they turn dark brown to black and often develop a greasy appearance. Lesions may coalesce, causing extensive foliar blighting, yellowing (chlorosis), and premature leaf drop. On green fruit, spots begin as small, slightly raised black blisters that enlarge up to 1/4 inch, becoming brown, scabby, rough, and sunken with irregular margins.

## Environmental Epidemiology
The bacteria survive on contaminated seed, volunteer tomato plants, and infected solanaceous crop debris. Infection and rapid disease spread are strongly favored by warm temperatures between 75°F and 86°F (24°C to 30°C) combined with high relative humidity, frequent rainfall, or overhead sprinkler irrigation. Bacteria enter through natural leaf openings (stomata) or physical wounds caused by wind, sandblasting, pruning, or insect feeding.

## Cultural Management Practices
1. **Certified Disease-Free Seed:** Use only certified pathogen-free seed. If seed source is uncertain, treat seed with hot water (122°F / 50°C for exactly 25 minutes) to eradicate internal and external bacterial contamination without damaging germination.
2. **Crop Rotation:** Implement a minimum 2-year crop rotation away from all solanaceous crops (tomatoes, peppers, potatoes, eggplants) to starve overwintering bacterial populations in the soil.
3. **Irrigation Management:** Avoid overhead irrigation. Use drip or trickle irrigation beneath plastic mulch to keep foliage dry.
4. **Sanitation:** Never work in tomato fields when foliage is wet from dew or rain, as bacteria are easily transferred on workers' hands, clothing, and machinery. Thoroughly sanitize wooden stakes and harvest bins using a 10% sodium hypochlorite solution or quaternary ammonium sanitizers.

## Chemical Control & Bactericide Strategy
Preventative bactericidal applications are essential because therapeutic cure of established bacterial infections is impossible:
- **Copper + Mancozeb Tank Mix:** Apply fixed copper bactericides (such as copper hydroxide or basic copper sulfate at 1.5 to 2.0 lb/acre) combined with an EBDC fungicide (such as mancozeb at 1.5 lb/acre). The combination releases more free copper ions, overcoming moderate levels of bacterial copper tolerance.
- **Plant Defense Activator:** Apply acibenzolar-S-methyl (Actigard 50WG at 0.33 to 0.75 oz/acre) to induce systemic acquired resistance (SAR). Begin Actigard applications preventatively after transplanting when plants are established, prior to disease onset.
- **Application Intervals:** Maintain a 7- to 10-day spray schedule under dry conditions, shortening intervals to 5 to 7 days during warm, rainy weather. Strictly observe label reentry intervals (REI) and pre-harvest intervals (PHI).
